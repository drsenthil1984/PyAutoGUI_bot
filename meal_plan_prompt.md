Indian / South Indian meals • Easy to prepare • Balanced nutrition • Office-friendly • Budget-conscious

The plan includes three main meals and two healthy snacks daily, with familiar home-cooked foods and minimal preparation.

## Weekly preparation for busy professionals

- Sunday (45–60 minutes): Wash and cut vegetables, boil chickpeas or rajma, prepare dosa batter, and portion nuts.
- Wednesday (20–30 minutes): Replenish vegetables and fruits, cook fresh dal, and prepare protein options.
- Every evening (10 minutes): Pack a snack and prepare ingredients for the following day's breakfast and lunch.
- Daily: Carry water, avoid sugary drinks, and take short walking breaks during desk work.

This is a general healthy-eating plan, not a fixed-calorie diet. Portion sizes need to reflect your weight, activity and health goals. If you take medication that can cause low blood sugar, meal timing and carbohydrate intake should be coordinated with your clinician.

## 3. Meal preparation guide

Breakfast preparation

10–15 min

Use pre-prepared idli/dosa batter, washed vegetables and previously cooked legumes. For oats or poha, sauté vegetables, add the grain and cook until tender; serve with curd for protein.

Lunch preparation

20–30 min

Use pre-soaked legumes, cooked dal or prepared curry bases. Make fresh rice or chapatis, reheat dal thoroughly, and pack vegetables and curd separately.

Dinner preparation

15–30 min

Prepare phulkas, a pre-chopped vegetable stir-fry and quick protein such as paneer, tofu, eggs or fish. Use a pressure cooker for dal and khichdi.

## 5. Daily routine and lifestyle recommendations

Hydration

Around 2–3 litres of beverages daily may be a reasonable starting range for many adults, but actual needs depend on climate, activity, health and food moisture. Drink regularly and adjust for thirst and medical advice.

Meal timing

Aim for breakfast around 8 AM, lunch around 1 PM and dinner around 7:30–8:30 PM, adjusting to your work schedule. Mid-morning and evening snacks can help if you are hungry between meals.

Physical activity

Stand and move briefly every 30–60 minutes. Aim toward at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise per week, with muscle-strengthening activity on two days.

Late-night meetings

Eat dinner before a long meeting when feasible. If dinner is delayed, use a planned snack such as plain curd, roasted chana or a boiled egg. Avoid replacing dinner with biscuits and sugary drinks.
